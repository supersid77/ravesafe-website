Harm reduction

Know before you go

No judgement. No lectures. Just the stuff that actually helps, so you and your mates can keep the night calm and safe while you're having fun.

The safest option is not using drugs. If you do choose to use, go slow, don't mix, and stay around people you trust.

Miss K's top tips

Little choices change the whole night

Especially now, when strength can be way higher than you're expecting. If you take one thing from this page: start small, give it time, keep your head, and look after each other.

Start low. Go slow.

Small amount first, then give it time to land. Most problems start with "these are shit" when it just hasn't kicked in yet, and topping up too soon.

If you can test, test

It's the quickest way to dodge nasty surprises, weird cuts and mis-sold pills and powders. Don't rely on "my mate said it's sound".

Sip. Don't chug.

Get out of the crowd, sit down and cool off. If you're sweating buckets, add electrolytes. Too much water too fast is as dangerous as too little.

Know the warning signs

Overheating, chest pain, extreme agitation, confusion, repeated vomiting, blue lips, a seizure, or being hard to wake. Call 999, tell staff and don't "wait and see".

Avoid mixing

Mixing turns a predictable night into a roulette wheel. Alcohol plus anything is where it gets messy fast, and downers with downers is where things go quiet.

Stay with safe people

Set a meeting point and check in. Lost your mates for more than 5 minutes? Go to the meeting point and stand somewhere visible. More eyes on you means you're less vulnerable.

MDMAEcstasy, molly, mandy. Pills, powder or crystals.

Can bring energy and closeness, but strength varies and mis-sold pills and powders are common. Effects usually start in 30 to 60 minutes and last 3 to 6 hours.

Main risks

  • Overheating in hot, crowded spaces
  • Getting water wrong: too little and too much are both dangerous
  • Serotonin syndrome: agitation, confusion, heavy sweating, shaking, high temperature
  • Pills with little or no MDMA, or something harmful instead

Don't mix with

  • Alcohol, cocaine or speed
  • Antidepressants (SSRIs, MAOIs) or other drugs that affect serotonin

Safer choices

  • Test if you can. Don't trust logos.
  • Start low and wait at least 2 hours before any redose, or skip it.
  • Cool down regularly. Sip water steadily, no more than about a pint an hour.

Call 999 for chest pain, collapse, a seizure, or someone who won't respond.

KetamineK, ket. Usually a white powder, snorted.

A dissociative anaesthetic. It can feel floaty, but it quickly knocks out your coordination and awareness of what's around you. Injecting is high risk.

Main risks

  • Falls and injuries, because you feel less pain
  • Choking if someone passes out or is sick
  • Bladder damage with frequent use
  • Passing out and breathing problems when mixed with downers

Don't mix with

  • Alcohol, benzos, opioids or nitrous

Safer choices

  • Sit down if you're wobbling.
  • Put anyone who's out of it on their side.
  • Take long breaks from regular use.

Don't leave them. If someone is slumped, hard to wake or breathing oddly, get help.

See it in the Ketaverse: Planet Drift
CocaineCoke, charlie. Short-acting stimulant.

Powerful and short-lived, which pushes people to keep topping up. It puts real strain on the heart.

Main risks

  • High blood pressure and an irregular heartbeat
  • Heart attack or stroke
  • Anxiety, paranoia and agitation
  • Dependence

Don't mix with

  • Alcohol. Together they make cocaethylene in the body, which puts extra strain on the heart.
  • MDMA or speed
  • Downers, which hide the danger signs

Safer choices

  • Set a limit before you start and stick to it.
  • Don't chase the high.
  • Know when to stop.

Call 999 for chest pain, a severe headache, collapse or a seizure.

See it in the Ketaverse: Planet KG
AlcoholThe one people forget is a drug.

A depressant that slows your reactions and judgement. Strength varies a lot between spirits, cocktails and strong cans. It kicks in within 10 to 30 minutes and keeps building for hours if you keep topping up.

Main risks

  • Alcohol poisoning: being sick, confusion, seizures, pale or blue skin, slow or irregular breathing, passing out
  • Heat and dehydration
  • Injuries and risky decisions

Don't mix with

  • Benzos, opioids or ketamine. Together they can slow breathing.
  • Cocaine or speed, which hide how drunk you are and strain the heart

Safer choices

  • Eat first and pace yourself.
  • Swap every other drink for a soft drink or water.
  • Set your limit early.

Call 999 if someone is hard to wake, breathing oddly or keeps being sick. Never leave them to "sleep it off" alone.

See it in the Ketaverse: Planet BevE
CannabisWeed, bud, edibles.

Can relax you or spike anxiety. Strength varies, and it affects coordination and judgement, especially with alcohol.

Main risks

  • Falls and accidents
  • Anxiety and paranoia, a higher risk for under-25s and anyone prone to psychosis
  • Edibles take a long time to kick in and are easy to overdo

Don't mix with

  • Alcohol, especially if you get anxious
  • Psychedelics or stimulants

Safer choices

  • With edibles, wait 1 to 2 hours before even thinking about more.
  • If a mate is panicking, take them somewhere calm and cool, reassure them and breathe slowly together.
  • Don't drive. You're more impaired than you think.
See it in the Ketaverse: Planet 420
SpeedAmphetamine. White or off-white powder or paste.

Keeps you going, but strains your heart, mood and sleep. Often cut with caffeine or fillers. Lasts 4 to 8 hours or more, with trouble sleeping afterwards.

Main risks

  • Racing pulse, high blood pressure and chest pain
  • Overheating and dehydration during long dancing
  • Comedown: low mood, irritability, anxiety
  • Binge cycles over days

Don't mix with

  • MDMA or cocaine
  • Alcohol, benzos or opioids

Safer choices

  • Take regular breaks and cool down.
  • Eat when you can.
  • Get welfare or medics early if someone is very agitated, confused or overheating.

Call 999 for chest pain, collapse, a seizure or someone you can't wake.

See it in the Ketaverse: Planet VibE
Pink cocaineTusi. A bright pink powder.

Usually not cocaine, and often not real 2C-B either. It's commonly an unknown mix, and no two batches are the same. That makes it high risk.

Main risks

  • Overdose, because you can't know the strength or what's in it
  • Uppers and downers in the same mix fighting each other
  • Medics can't easily treat what they can't identify

Don't mix with

  • Alcohol, stimulants or depressants

Safer choices

  • If you can't check what it is, the safest choice is not to take it.
  • Test if you can.

Call 999 if someone gets worse quickly.

See it in the Ketaverse: Planet Rubble
2C-BSynthetic psychedelic. Pills or powder.

Visuals plus body effects. The dose matters a lot, and pills can be mis-sold. Snorting it is very painful and hits hard.

Main risks

  • Anxiety, panic and paranoia, especially somewhere chaotic
  • Being overwhelmed if a pill is stronger than expected
  • More confusion when mixed

Don't mix with

  • MDMA, alcohol or ketamine

Safer choices

  • If you're new, swallow rather than snort, start low and be patient.
  • Wait before redosing.
  • Be with people you trust and move somewhere calmer if it gets heavy.
LSDAcid. Blotter tabs, liquid or gel tabs.

A strong psychedelic that lasts a long time: it starts in 30 to 90 minutes and often runs 8 to 12 hours or more. Your mindset and where you are matter a lot.

Main risks

  • Panic and bad trips
  • Accidents from poor judgement
  • Bringing on or worsening mental health problems

Don't mix with

  • Alcohol, stimulants or lots of cannabis

Safer choices

  • Start low and be somewhere calm with people you trust.
  • If someone's overwhelmed, find a quiet space, keep your voice calm and breathe slowly with them.

Call 999 for chest pain, a seizure, collapse or someone who won't respond.

See it in the Ketaverse: Planet Woah
Magic mushroomsPsilocybin. Shrooms.

Visuals and waves of emotion, but they can also bring on sickness and overwhelm, especially somewhere chaotic. They start in 20 to 60 minutes and often last 4 to 6 hours. Strength varies by type.

Main risks

  • Panic, paranoia and feeling trapped in your thoughts
  • Being sick, with a choking risk if very out of it
  • Higher mental health risk if you or your family have a history of psychosis

Don't mix with

  • Alcohol, lots of cannabis or stimulants

Safer choices

  • Start low somewhere comfortable with someone you trust.
  • If a mate's spiralling, cut the noise, stay calm and remind them it will pass.

Call 999 if they're unsafe, won't respond or have a seizure.

See it in the Ketaverse: Planet Myco
Nitrous oxideNOS, balloons, laughing gas.

Short and floaty. The main dangers are lack of oxygen, sudden dizziness and falls. Heavy use can drain your vitamin B12 and damage your nerves.

Main risks

  • Blacking out and getting hurt
  • Frostbite-type burns from breathing it straight from a canister
  • Nerve damage from heavy use: tingling, numbness, weakness

Don't mix with

  • Ketamine, alcohol or benzos

Safer choices

  • Never breathe it straight from a canister.
  • Sit down and take long breaks between balloons.
  • Stop at any tingling or numbness.

Call 999 if someone won't respond or isn't breathing normally.

BenzosBenzodiazepines. Street pills and prescription sedatives.

Prescribed for anxiety and sleep. Used recreationally they're high risk: blackouts are common and mixing can kill.

Main risks

  • Breathing slowing down when mixed with other downers
  • Blackouts: someone can seem awake but remember nothing
  • Street pills can contain anything, at any strength
  • Severe withdrawal, including seizures

Don't mix with

  • Alcohol, opioids or ketamine

Safer choices

  • Don't share pills or assume you know what's in them.
  • Never leave someone alone. If they're being sick, keep them on their side.

Call 999 for extreme drowsiness, slow or odd breathing, blue lips or someone who won't wake.

DMTCrystals, vapes or herbal blends (changa).

Very intense with a very fast onset. Smoked or vaped it's short. Taken as ayahuasca or by mouth it lasts much longer. It needs a calm setting and someone sober-ish with you.

Main risks

  • Overwhelm and panic somewhere noisy
  • Disorientation and falls if no one's with you
  • Dangerous interactions with MAOIs and some medications

Don't mix with

  • Alcohol or stimulants
  • Any medication you're unsure about. If in doubt, don't.

Safer choices

  • Keep it calm and away from crowds.
  • Have someone with you who's sober enough to help.

Get medics or call 999 if someone becomes distressed, unsafe or won't respond.

Heroin and opioidsIncluding strong painkillers.

Opioids slow your breathing. Overdose risk is high, especially with alcohol or benzos.

Overdose signs

  • Slow, shallow or stopped breathing
  • Blue or grey lips or fingertips
  • Cold, clammy skin
  • Unconscious

Don't mix with

  • Benzos, alcohol or ketamine

Safer choices

  • Never use alone.
  • Carry naloxone if you can get it, and know how to use it.

Call 999 straight away for overdose signs. Say you think it's an opioid overdose, use naloxone if you have it, and keep checking their breathing.

Quick reality check

Staff are there to deal with emergencies, but carrying drugs can still get you into trouble. Get help early and keep the focus on safety. You don't need to be a medic to help.
