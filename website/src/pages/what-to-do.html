Festival first aid for mates

What to do

Real steps for real moments. You don't need to be a medic. Stay calm, get help early and don't leave them on their own.

Start here

Miss K's 60-second check

  1. Is it safe to go to them?

    Look for crowd crush, traffic, glass or fire. Don't become a second casualty. If it isn't safe, get staff.

  2. Do they respond?

    Say their name loudly and tap their shoulders. Ask them to open their eyes.

  3. Are they breathing normally?

    Tilt the head back, lift the chin, and look, listen and feel for up to 10 seconds. Gasping or snoring-like breaths are not normal breathing.

No response and not breathing normally

Call 999 on speaker and start CPR. See Not responding.

No response but breathing normally

Put them in the recovery position and get medics. Keep checking their breathing.

Awake but not right

Stay with them, find the situation above and get medics early. If you're unsure, go early and get help.

Keeps their airway clear

The recovery position

For someone who isn't responding but is breathing normally. It stops them choking if they're sick.

Video won't play? Watch it on YouTube.

  1. Arm out

    Kneel beside them. Put the arm nearest you out at a right angle, palm up.

  2. Hand to cheek

    Bring their far arm across their chest and hold the back of their hand against the cheek nearest you.

  3. Knee up and roll

    Bend their far knee up. Pull on the knee to roll them towards you onto their side.

  4. Open the airway

    Tilt the head back and lift the chin so they can breathe. Keep checking their breathing until help arrives.

Situation guides

Step by step

Not responding

Do this

  • Shout for help. Call 999 on speaker so your hands are free.
  • Send someone for the festival medics and a defibrillator (AED).
  • Check breathing for up to 10 seconds.
  • Not breathing normally: start CPR. Push hard and fast in the centre of the chest, about 5 to 6cm deep, 100 to 120 times a minute. The 999 call handler will talk you through it.
  • When the defib arrives, switch it on and do what it says.
  • Breathing normally: put them in the recovery position and keep checking.

Don't

  • Leave them alone, even for a minute.
  • Give them anything to eat or drink.
  • Stop CPR until help takes over or they start breathing normally.

Seizure

Do this

  • Move anything hard or sharp away from them.
  • Cushion their head with a jacket or bag.
  • Time it from start to finish.
  • When it stops, check their breathing and put them in the recovery position.
  • Stay until they're fully awake. At a festival, get the medics either way.

Don't

  • Hold them down or put anything in their mouth.

Call 999 if

  • It lasts 5 minutes or more
  • Another one starts
  • They're injured
  • They have breathing problems afterwards
  • They don't wake up properly

Too hot

Do this

  • Get them out of the crowd into shade or a cooler spot.
  • Take off extra layers.
  • Cool them down: fan them, sponge or spray with water, cool packs on the neck, armpits and groin.
  • Small sips of water, only if they're fully awake.
  • Stay with them and get medics.

Don't

  • Let them gulp lots of water fast. Too much too quickly is dangerous too.

Call 999 if

  • They're confused or acting strangely
  • They collapse
  • They have a seizure
  • Their skin feels very hot

Drunk or drugged

Do this

  • If they're very drowsy or being sick, put them in the recovery position.
  • Keep checking their breathing.
  • Keep any wraps, pills or packets to show the medics.
  • Tell medics honestly what was taken and when. They're there to help, not judge.

Don't

  • Leave them to "sleep it off" on their own.
  • Make them sick, or give coffee or a cold shower.
  • Give them more of anything.

Call 999 on speaker if

  • You can't wake them properly
  • Their breathing is slow, noisy or odd
  • They have a seizure
  • They're very hot and confused
  • You think they've been poisoned or spiked

Panic or bad trip

Do this

  • Move somewhere quieter and calmer, away from the crowd and lights.
  • Speak low and slow: "You're safe. I'm with you. This will pass."
  • Breathe slowly with them. In for 4, out for 6.
  • Take them to welfare. You don't need to be in danger to go.

Don't

  • Leave them alone, laugh, or argue with what they're seeing.

Get medics or call 999 if

  • They have chest pain
  • They collapse
  • They're very hot or confused
  • Their breathing won't slow down

Asthma

Do this

  • Sit them up straight and loosen anything tight.
  • Help them use their reliever inhaler (usually blue). One puff every 30 to 60 seconds, up to 10 puffs.
  • Keep calm and keep them calm.

Call 999 if

  • They're no better after 10 puffs
  • It's getting worse
  • They're too breathless to talk
  • They don't have their inhaler
Back to the list

Severe allergic reaction

Do this

  • Call 999 and say "anaphylaxis".
  • If they carry an adrenaline auto-injector (like an EpiPen), help them use it in the outer thigh.
  • Lie them down with legs raised. If breathing is hard, let them sit up instead.
  • If they're no better after 5 minutes and have a second injector, use it.

Signs to look for

  • Swelling of the lips, tongue or throat
  • Trouble breathing or wheezing
  • Feeling faint or collapsing
  • A rash spreading fast

Head knock

Do this

  • Sit them down and hold something cold wrapped in a cloth on the bump.
  • Keep an eye on them and don't let them wander off alone.
  • Get them checked by medics. Drink and drugs can hide the warning signs.

Call 999 if

  • They were knocked out, even briefly
  • They have a seizure
  • They keep being sick
  • Their headache is getting worse
  • They're confused or very sleepy
  • They have weakness or numbness anywhere
Back to the list

Burns

Do this

  • Cool it under cool or lukewarm running water for 20 minutes.
  • Take off jewellery or clothing near the burn, unless it's stuck.
  • Cover it loosely with cling film or a clean, non-fluffy dressing.
  • Get medics to look at anything bigger than their hand, or on the face, hands, feet or groin.

Don't

  • Use ice, creams or butter.
  • Pop blisters.

Be honest. It saves time.

What to tell them

Calling 999

  1. Where you are. Stage, campsite, landmark or anything you can see.
  2. What happened.
  3. Are they breathing normally?
  4. Are they awake, drowsy or not responding?
  5. Any big risks, like a seizure, chest pain or very hot skin.
  6. Rough age and sex.
  7. What they've taken and when, if you know.
  8. What you're doing right now.

Handing over to festival medics

  1. What's happened, in order, with rough times.
  2. Their symptoms and how they've changed.
  3. What they've taken and the time of the last dose.
  4. Any medical conditions or medication they take.
  5. Any injuries.
  6. What you've already done.
  7. Their name, ID or wristband details if you have them.

Medics are there to help, not judge. The truth gets your mate the right care faster.
