Quick wins

Do these before you leave and you'll avoid most problems.

  1. Choose a meeting point. Phones die. You don't.
  2. Arrive fed and hydrated. Start steady. Keep it steady.

Knowing who to go to is half the battle

How support works at festivals

Welfare

Quiet space. Trained listeners. No judgement.

  • Anxiety spike
  • Bad trip
  • Overwhelmed, crying, panicky
  • You just need to sit somewhere safe

You don't need to be "in danger" to go to welfare.

Medical

Paramedics. Nurses. Doctors. Proper help.

  • Chest pain
  • Seizure
  • Breathing trouble
  • Unconscious, can't be woken properly

Go early. You are not wasting their time.

Stewards

Directions and info. They're your shortcut.

  • Where is welfare?
  • Where is water?
  • Where is the nearest exit?

Security

Immediate safety response.

  • Aggression
  • You feel unsafe
  • Someone won't leave you alone

One rule: if you're unsure, ask staff. Getting help early keeps things small.

Getting split up happens

Lost mate plan

Panic makes it worse. Use a plan.

What you do

  1. Go to the meeting point.
  2. Wait 10 minutes.
  3. Call once, then stop spamming.
  4. Go to the backup point.
  5. Still no joy? Check welfare.

What to tell staff

  1. Their name or nickname.
  2. What they're wearing.
  3. Where you last saw them.
  4. What time you last saw them.
  5. Anything to flag, like a medical risk.
If they might be in danger

If someone may be in danger or needs urgent medical help, tell staff or call 999 straight away. Don't wait at meeting points. A calm plan beats a chaotic group chat.

Kit lists

Pick the list that matches your weekend.

Day ticket kit listFull day out. Home after.

Essentials

  • Ticket and ID
  • Fully charged phone and power bank
  • Bank card and a bit of cash
  • Travel plan, including how you get home

Hydration and fuel

  • Reusable water bottle
  • Snacks that actually fill you up
  • Electrolyte sachet if it's hot or you're dancing loads

Comfort and basics

  • Sunscreen
  • Sunglasses and a hat
  • Jumper or light jacket
  • Tissues and hand sanitiser
  • Plasters and pain relief
  • Condoms if you might need them
  • Period supplies

If you party

  • Drug testing kit
  • Chewing gum or boiled sweets
  • Your own straw, never shared
  • A small note with emergency contacts, allergies and meds

Miss K says: you don't need a suitcase. You just need to be smart.

Full weekend camping kit listThree days. Little sleep. Worth it.

Festival essentials

  • Ticket, ID and booking confirmations
  • Cash and cards, split between your wallet and a hidden backup
  • Phone and charger cable
  • Power bank (10,000 to 20,000mAh) and a spare cable
  • Travel plan, including taxi numbers
  • Festival address and postcode
  • Map and set times screenshots
  • Emergency contacts written down

Sleeping setup

  • Tent, tested at home first
  • Extra pegs and a mallet
  • Groundsheet or tarp
  • Sleeping bag, 3-season or comfort rated 5 to 10°C
  • Roll mat or airbed and pump
  • Earplugs and eye mask
  • Head torch and spare batteries

Safety

  • Basic first aid bits and blister plasters
  • Your meds, packed dry, plus extra
  • Suncream and after-sun
  • A whistle

Make your RaveSafe ID

Make this once, then set it as your phone's lock screen for the weekend. If your phone's found or you're unwell, it tells people what to do. Everything you type stays on your phone. Nothing is sent or saved by us.

Privacy: only include medical details you're happy showing on a locked phone. Don't share the image publicly or in group chats. Share meeting points with your group separately.
